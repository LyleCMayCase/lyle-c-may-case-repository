California v. Trombetta

CASE ENTRY

Last Revised • July 9, 2026

This Source Verification Page documents California v. Trombetta, 467 U.S. 479 (1984), a decision of the Supreme Court of the United States cited in Claim I of the Supplemental Motion. The Court established that due process requires the preservation of evidence possessing apparent exculpatory value when that evidence cannot reasonably be obtained by other available means. The decision provides the constitutional framework governing claims involving the preservation of materially exculpatory evidence.

Cited to Support

Lyle C. May alleges that law enforcement agencies involved in the investigation of his case failed to properly preserve, maintain, account for, or disclose physical evidence and that deficiencies in the preservation and handling of evidence warrant further judicial review. Claim I cites California v. Trombetta, 467 U.S. 479 (1984), for the constitutional principle that due process requires the preservation of evidence possessing apparent exculpatory value when that evidence cannot reasonably be obtained through other available means.

The decision establishes the constitutional framework governing claims involving materially exculpatory evidence and its preservation by law enforcement. May relies upon this authority in support of his allegations that the handling of physical evidence during the investigation and prosecution in Asheville, Buncombe County, North Carolina, impaired his ability to challenge the State's evidence and warrants further judicial scrutiny.
