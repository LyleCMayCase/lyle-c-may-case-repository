State v. Brown

CASE ENTRY

Last Revised • July 9, 2026

This Source Verification Page documents the North Carolina Court of Appeals' decision in State v. Brown, cited in Claim I of the Supplemental Motion. The opinion addresses evidentiary chain-of-custody requirements and is cited in support of Lyle C. May's claim that deficiencies in the collection, preservation, storage, and documentation of physical evidence raise substantial questions regarding its integrity and reliability.

Cited to Support

Lyle C. May's reliance upon State v. Brown, 101 N.C. App. 71, 398 S.E.2d 905 (1990), for the legal principle that a sufficient chain of custody must be established when physical evidence is susceptible to alteration, contamination, or substitution and questions have been raised regarding its integrity. May cites Brown in support of his allegations that documented deficiencies in the collection, storage, preservation, and inventory of evidence by the Asheville Police Department and the Buncombe County Sheriff's Office raise substantial questions regarding the integrity and reliability of physical evidence in his case.
