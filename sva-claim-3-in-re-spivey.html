In re Spivey

CASE ENTRY

Last Revised • July 11, 2026

This Source Verification Page documents the North Carolina Supreme Court decision in In re Spivey, cited in Claim III of the Supplemental Motion. The decision is relied upon for the principle that North Carolina courts possess inherent authority to appoint independent counsel when necessary for the proper administration of justice. It is included to support the request that independent counsel be appointed to investigate, develop, and present evidence concerning the allegations discussed in Paragraph 128 of the Supplemental Motion.

Cited to Support

Lyle C. May alleges that North Carolina courts possess inherent authority to appoint independent counsel when necessary for the proper administration of justice. Specifically, May cites In re Spivey as an example in which the North Carolina Supreme Court approved a superior court's appointment of independent counsel to gather and present evidence in a judicial proceeding involving allegations of misconduct by a district attorney. May relies upon this authority to support his request that the Court appoint independent counsel to investigate, develop, and present evidence concerning allegations of prosecutorial and law-enforcement misconduct arising from his Buncombe County case and the July 1997 murders in Asheville, North Carolina.
